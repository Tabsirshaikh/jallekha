# The tanker water problem: how it works today, where it fails, who it hurts

Research compiled 6 October 2026 for JalLekha (PS11, HMWSSB). **Problem only; no solution is proposed here.**
It builds on, and corrects where needed, two earlier notes: `jallekha_tanker_problem_research.md`
(national picture and incident register) and `jallekha_geographical_research.md` (places).

Hyderabad is the focus because HMWSSB is the client. Other cities appear where they show the same
workflow failing in a way that is documented.

## 1. Summary of findings

1. **Hyderabad's piped supply has not grown with the city.** Daily supply has stayed at about
   550 MGD for ten years while water connections rose about 70% (8 lakh to 13.8 lakh since 2015),
   according to the HMWSSB managing director [R] NEWS. The CAG found the Board supplied only about
   66-71 litres per person per day against a 150 lpcd norm, with 39% losses (audit of 2013-18) [S] REAL.
   Tankers fill the gap.
2. **Tanker demand has roughly tripled in four years and now runs all year.** HMWSSB's own open data
   shows March-April bookings of 128,338 in 2022 and 421,285 in 2025; May 2026 had 319,106 bookings
   [R] REAL-COMPUTED. Demand even rose during rains in June-July 2026 [R] NEWS.
3. **The official workflow treats a booking as the measure of need.** A household with a consumer
   account, a phone, Rs 500 and a sump books; the booking goes to the nearest filling station and
   waits in arrival order. The MD describes the booking system as how the Board knows "the daily water
   requirements of every apartment, building, and locality" [R] NEWS. People who cannot book are not
   in the system at all.
4. **When demand exceeds capacity, the arrival-order queue fails.** There are documented cases: a 10-day
   wait in Kondapur, Manikonda, Serilingampally and Nizampet against a 42-hour target (2016); 23,000
   pending requests (2019); only half of demand delivered within 24 hours (2024); 15,200 bookings in one
   day against about 12,000 supplied (30 May 2026). In June-July 2026 the official data itself shows
   **63,428 bookings not delivered (Jun-Aug)**, the first months on record where the delivered share
   fell below 93% [R] REAL-COMPUTED.
5. **Supply goes to where people can book, not only to where need is.** About 70% of tankers go to
   western areas (HMWSSB via NEWS, April 2026). The MD has said "nearly 90%" of demand comes from west
   Hyderabad (2025). Bookings are very unequal across sections (Gini about 0.77, top 10% of section
   labels about 62% of bookings, Mar-Aug 2026, REAL-COMPUTED with a caveat on section re-cuts).
6. **"Free" tankers for unconnected slums are often not free in practice.** Residents report paying the
   driver, or seeing the tanker stop coming (2017, 2019) [R] NEWS. Vigilance found a tanker scam in the
   Old City (45 criminal cases) [S] NEWS.
7. **Proof that a trip happened is weak.** Smart cards and GPS were announced in 2016. In April 2026 the
   Board said tokens would "soon" carry barcodes for trip-by-trip tracking [R] NEWS. Private tankers have
   used the HMWSSB logo [R] NEWS. Complaints have been closed without field visits; memos were issued to
   11 managers [S] NEWS.
8. **The same weaknesses show up in other cities and in rural scarcity relief**, with worse outcomes:
   deaths in queue fights (Delhi 2018), prohibitory orders at filling points (Latur 2016), tankers
   ordered away from VIPs by a High Court (Shimla 2018), audit findings of inequitable supply and no GPS
   (Delhi CAG, tabled 2026), filling-point records showing 18 of about 50 tankers (Pune 2026), and only
   about 10% of private tankers registered when a city tried to regulate them (Bengaluru 2024).

## 3. Where tankers are used

### 3.1 National

Carried over from the earlier note: NSS 76th round (2018) lists tankers as an improved source; public
tanker as the principal drinking source for 262 sampled households (0.2%, unweighted) and supplementary
for 1,514 (1.4%) [C][S] REAL. No newer national count was found. Tanker dependence is concentrated in
unconnected urban settlements, fast-growing city edges and drought-hit villages, not spread evenly.

## 5. Faults in the Hyderabad workflow, with cases

Each fault: what is wrong, the evidence, and a case where it showed.

### Fault 1: bookings are treated as need, so people who cannot book are invisible

- The Board plans from bookings (MD quote, step 1) [R] NEWS.
- Booking needs a phone, a consumer account, money up front and a sump. In Census 2011, households
  without drinking water on their premises reach 33.4% in some GHMC wards (median 5.5%), and households
  with no phone reach 19.2% (PROJECT, from Census HH-14 tables).
- The free-water scheme (20,000 litres a month from January 2021) reached landowners, not tenants:
  "consumers residing in rented houses would be unable to avail of the offer" [S] NEWS.
- **Case: Dasaram Basti, SR Nagar (June 2019).** An unnotified slum of about 300 people, 5 km from the
  Chief Minister's office. No connection; five public taps supplied once in two or three days for about an
  hour. Women start at 4 am. Saroja needs 20 pots a day and manages about 10; apartment owners charge Rs 2
  a pot. Residents say free tankers stopped coming after a political dispute. The corporator said nothing
  could be done until a land case ends [R] NEWS.

### Fault 2: the arrival-order queue breaks when demand exceeds capacity

| Year | What happened | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 2016 | Kondapur, Manikonda, Serilingampally, Nizampet: 10-day wait against a 42-hour target. A Nanakramguda resident: "Whenever my daughter calls customer care, they say we will have to wait for two more days." The Board extended station hours to 9.30 pm and asked general managers to phone citizens | [R] NEWS |
| Jul 2019 | About 23,000 tanker requests pending at one point; the city was drawing from dead storage of Nagarjuna Sagar | [R] NEWS |
| 2024 | HMWSSB official: "we were only able to deliver about 50 per cent of the demand within 24 hours" | [S] NEWS |
| 30 May 2026 | 15,200 bookings in a day (previous record 12,000) against about 12,000 supplied; fleet already 1,250 | [R] NEWS |
| Jun-Aug 2026 | Open data: 23,095 (Jun), 27,825 (Jul) and 12,508 (Aug) bookings not delivered; delivered share 89-92% | [R] REAL-COMPUTED |

The official monthly "delivered" figure hides most of this. In 2024, the year an official said only
about half of demand arrived within 24 hours, the open data shows 99.7-100% delivered every month,
because a booking that arrives days late still counts as delivered. No waiting time is published.

### Fault 3: supply follows the people who book most, not measured need

- About 70% of tankers go west (2026); "nearly 90% of demand" from the west (MD, 2025) [R] NEWS.
- Bookings across section labels, Mar-Aug 2026: Gini about 0.77; the top 10% of labels took about 62%,
  the bottom half about 2.9% [R] REAL-COMPUTED. Caveat: sections were re-cut in Feb-Mar 2026 and the data
  has "(OLD)" and "(NEW)" labels for the same areas, so these are label-level figures. On the stable
  2022-Feb 2024 sections the project found Gini 0.78, top 10% 67.7%, bottom half 2.9% (PROJECT).
- The western wards are also short of groundwater and piped supply (Section 2), so heavy booking there is
  partly real need. The point is that booking volume mixes need with the ability to book.
- **Case: 2019 RTI.** NTR Nagar 24,052 domestic trips; Madhapur circles 22,960 and 23,433; Erragadda
  22,100; Banjara Hills 19,741; Jubilee Hills 20,051 commercial trips. Over the same period Old City
  colonies were buying "free" water by the drum (Fault 4) [R] NEWS.

### Fault 4: the free supply for unconnected areas is sold at the point of delivery

- **Case: Old City colonies (March 2017).** Water for colonies without connections "is being sold for
  Rs 10 to Rs 30 per drum". Millat Nagar resident: "If we fail to pay the driver, he will not show up for
  the next week. Then we have to keep requesting the officials and local politicians." A local leader
  called it "organised loot" [R] NEWS.
- **Case: slums across the city (June 2019).** Kamala Nagar (Chaderghat), Mairaj Colony (Tolichowki),
  BS and MS Maqtha (Begumpet), Talla Basti (West Anandbagh): residents pooled money for the "free" truck
  and paid up to Rs 1,000 for a 5,000-litre trip; trucks took more than three days to answer. HMWSSB's
  technical director said he knew tankers charged money and asked citizens to complain [R] NEWS.
- Vigilance busted a tanker scam in the Old City: 45 criminal cases; the Board lost about Rs 50 lakh a
  month (year not confirmed) [S] NEWS, UNVERIFIED year.

### Fault 5: no independent proof that a trip reached the right house

- The Board's own controls are at the filling station (smart card 2016, CCTV 2026) and on the vehicle
  (GPS). Trip-level tracking by barcode was still "soon" in April 2026 [R] NEWS.
- No step was found where the resident confirms delivery.
- **Case: fake HMWSSB tankers (2026).** "Some private tanker owners were illegally displaying the
  official logo"; one was seized and a criminal case booked [R] NEWS.
- **Case: Board water from private sources.** SaciWATERs field research found HMWSSB "sometimes" buys
  water from private tankers, whose water may already be exposed to rust [R] (research blog, 2017).

### Fault 6: complaints are closed, not resolved

- **Case: Boduppal.** One resident raised low-pressure tickets 6013 (3 Feb), 5925 (7 Mar), 3657 (9 Apr),
  5685 (6 Jun) and 4645 (1 Jul); each was marked resolved with no inspection [S] NEWS.
- After press reports, memos were issued to 11 managers found to have resolved complaints without visiting
  the premises [S] NEWS (year to confirm).
- 2019: an MS Maqtha resident asked several times to change the supply schedule and "got no response"
  [R] NEWS.

### Fault 7: the system has no fallback and no memory

- **Case: data centre outage (from Thursday 31 July 2025).** Tanker booking and billing went down for
  three days; only 1,200 tanker trips were completed by Friday evening; long queues followed. Staff ran
  booking manually [S] NEWS.
- A missed or late booking gets no priority the next day; the queue is rebuilt from new bookings (no
  carry-forward rule was found in any source).

### Fault 8: the private market fills the gap, unregulated

| Issue | Evidence | Mark |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Private 10 KL tanker Rs 1,500-2,000; 25 KL Rs 3,500-4,000 (2025); about Rs 1,000-1,200 for 5,000 litres (2024). Kondapur families paying about Rs 1,500 a month (2016) | [S] NEWS; [R] NEWS (2016) |
| Illegal groundwater | Revenue officials confiscated about 42 tankers drawing illegally at Narsingi; Shamshabad, Shamirpet, Hayathnagar and Narsingi called hubs, about 250 trips from these areas (year not confirmed). WALTA Act 2002 limits borewell depth to 400 feet; rigs go beyond 1,000 feet. FIR over illegal borewells inside Himayat Sagar's full tank level and buffer zone | [S] NEWS |
| Quality | Private sources store water in plastic-lined pits for two years, uncovered; mild-steel tankers rust if water stands over 20 minutes and are repainted inside every six months. Residents of peri-urban Hyderabad drink this water and report body aches | [R] research blog (SaciWATERs) |

### Fault 9: the data used to judge the system is thin or inconsistent

- The open data publishes monthly booked and delivered counts only (Section 4, step 11) [R] REAL.
- Two official-sourced May 2026 figures disagree (319,106 vs 3.36 lakh) [R].
- 57 piezometers for the whole city (Section 2.1) [R] NEWS.
- The CAG found 82% of consumer accounts with no working meter [S] REAL, so nobody knows how much piped
  water each area actually received before it booked a tanker.

## 8. Facts any solution will have to deal with

These are constraints from the evidence, not design choices.

1. **Need is not observed.** The only city-wide signal is bookings. Census 2011 is the last ward-level
   source on water access and phones; groundwater has 57 city piezometers; most connections are unmetered.
2. **Demand is seasonal and growing.** About 4.5 times between April and October; March-April roughly
   tripled from 2022 to 2025. A daily capacity binds in peak months (June-July 2026 is the first time it
   shows in the open data).
3. **There are two populations with different failures.** Booked, paying customers (mostly apartments
   and the west) fail on delay. Unconnected slums and tail-end colonies fail on access, informal payment
   and no record.
4. **The fleet is not the only limit.** Filling stations, their hours and their location (15 of 84 carry
   the peak) are choke points.
5. **Delivery records come from the supplier side.** Smart cards, CCTV and GPS watch the tanker. No source
   shows the resident confirming.
6. **The private market is the fallback.** It is unregistered, draws groundwater illegally in places, and
   its quality is unknown. When the public queue fails, people move there and pay several times more.
7. **Languages and channels.** 47% of bookings come by phone (IVRS), 46% by app and 7% by web (April 2026),
   so voice matters. The language mix of the district comes from a secondary source (Telugu 43.35%, Urdu
   43.24%; see `docs/research/P4.md`).
8. **Official figures disagree and wait times are not published.** Any comparison needs one stated source.
