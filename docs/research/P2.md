## Data: newer HMWSSB tanker table (affects A1, A8, and also P1/P3)

- **Source:** [Telangana Open Data Portal, dataset 7f408a3a-…](https://data.telangana.gov.in/api/1/metastore/schemas/dataset/items/7f408a3a-7cdb-4d33-bfa3-1869f88c0e25) [R], "Open Government License, India", modified 2026-09-06. There are 56 monthly CSVs (`tankers_reports_YYYY_M.csv`) with the columns `year, month, division, section, noofbookings, delivered`. I found it through [neer-vazhvu PR #435](https://github.com/SundareshPrasanna/neer-vazhvu/pull/435) [R].
- **Checked by download (all 56 files):** 5,356,766 bookings and 5,281,651 delivered, Jan 2022-Aug 2026. Dec 2022 has no rows (as in D1). **All 26 months that overlap D1 match `data/derived/equity/monthly.csv` exactly** (bookings and delivered).
- **Demand:** Mar-Jul 2023 = 397,881 bookings; Mar-Jul 2025 = 977,933. Peak month: 319,106 in May 2026.
- **Capacity now binds:** the delivered share is ≥ 98% in every month up to May 2026, then **92.1% / 89.2% / 92.4%** in Jun / Jul / Aug 2026. That is 63,428 bookings not delivered, and the largest gaps are Boduppal, Madhapur and Shamshiguda (Jul 2026). The portal gives no reason for them.
- **Section re-cut:** no Feb or Mar 2026 section name matches a 2025 name; rows jump from 175 to 311 in Mar 2026; later files mix new rows with rows marked "(OLD)". 2026 must not be compared section by section with earlier years. 2023 and 2025 share 168 of 178 sections.
- **Ownership:** data intake is P1's area (S2/D1). This note only records the finding; ingesting the data is a handoff (below).

## F8 Fair queue

- **Today's process** ([HMWSSB tanker tracking page](https://tanker.hyderabadwater.gov.in/TANKERTRACKING) [R]): "Token will be assigned to nearest Tanker Filling Station"; the queue "shows service requests waiting against the assigned Tanker Filling Station and serial number of token in the waiting list". Each token carries a **CAN** (consumer account number).
- **Caps** ([HMWSSB guidelines](https://hyderabadwater.gov.in/en/index.php/services/customers-services/tanker-booking-guidelines) [R]):
  - Domestic: 1 per 2 days or 8 per month, Rs 500.
  - Apartments with 5-24 flats: 2 per day or 30 per month, Rs 500.
  - Apartments with 25-200 flats: 3 per day or 60 per month, Rs 500.
  - Commercial: 1 per day or 30 per month, Rs 850.
- **Channels:** 47% IVRS, 46% app, 7% website ([Siasat, 2026-04-23](https://www.siasat.com/barcodes-cctv-two-shift-deliveries-how-hmwssb-is-managing-tanker-deliveries-3459204/) [R]). Phone access matters (CAD).
- **Other cities:** all use FCFS with caps and none publishes a need priority.
  - Chennai Dial for Water: daily quota, first come first served, online payment barrier ([Citizen Matters, 2019](https://citizenmatters.in/dial-for-water-scheme-cmwssb-chennai-water-crisis/) [R]; [tnliv](https://tnliv.in/metrowater-online-booking/) [S]).
  - Bengaluru Sanchari Cauvery: booked 24 h ahead, OTP at delivery, priority only for households awaiting a connection ([ETV Bharat, 2025-05-11](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!state/karnataka-govt-offers-4000-litre-tankers-at-rs-660-with-24-hour-delivery-assurance-enn25051103573) [S]).
- **Decision:**
  - B1 = FCFS per filling station, each station with a daily trip capacity.
  - JL keeps tiers 0/1, then deficit round robin across sections (later wards) **inside each station's capacity**.
  - Caps by category go in `config/allocation.py` with the URL above.
  - The `order_bookings` signature is fixed in A3 for P3's T7.
- **Open:** whether a household **without a CAN** (no connection) can book at all is **not verified**. If it cannot, those households are invisible to F8 and only F9 reaches them. Ask HMWSSB customer care (155313) or the owner.

## F9 Scheduled supply

- Delhi publishes tanker schedules to "predetermined fixed points" in localities without piped water, chosen with local representatives ([DJB](https://delhijalboard.delhi.gov.in/jalboard/schedule-water-tankers) [S]). This supports officer-registered points (list starts empty) and a published timetable.
- Trucking is expensive and should be short-term ([Oxfam WASH](https://www.oxfamwash.org/water-trucking-in-drought-emergencies/) [S]; [ODI HPN](https://odihpn.org/en/publication/rethinking-emergency-water-provision-can-we-stop-direct-water-trucking-in-the-same-places-every-year/) [S]). The report shows recurring scheduled points as a signal for a connection, not as a solution.
- **Keep** weighted max-min with a minimum-share floor (Gao et al.). Sinclair et al. show an unavoidable trade-off between fairness (envy) and efficiency, i.e. unused capacity ([Operations Research 71(5):1689-1705](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/opre.2022.2397) [S]; [arXiv 2105.05308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.05308) [S]). **Add:** report unused litres next to fairness, so the cost of the floor is visible.

- **Fleet:**
  - 900 → 1,200 tankers; same-day delivery replaced about 3 days; private price about Rs 2,000 per 5,000 l ([Deccan Chronicle, 2026-02-24](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/tanker-demand-dips-as-groundwater-improves-hmwssb-increases-fleet-to-ensure-faster-supply-1939389) [R]).

  - Capacity scenarios use these figures as ASSUMED, with the source.
- **Related work:** Gudi, Subbiah & Laspidou, tanker scheduling under uncertainty in Bengaluru ([Ind. Eng. Chem. Res., doi 10.1021/acs.iecr.3c00496](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acs.iecr.3c00496) [S]). It has no fairness objective. Cited as related work; we do not adopt its method.

## F19 Evaluation against FCFS

- **Replay:**
  - Main: Mar-Jul 2025, with monthly section totals exact (A1 test) and daily arrivals SYNTHETIC.
  - Secondary: Mar-Jul 2023.
  - Capacity scenarios: (a) fleet × trips/day (ASSUMED); (b) tightened until the delivered share equals the REAL Jun-Aug 2026 level of about 89-92%.
- **Seeds:** every policy runs on the **same seeds** (common random numbers), so differences come from the policy and not the noise. Use 30+ seeds and report the mean and range.
- **Measures (vs B1):**
  - Unmet need share and waiting days.
  - Tanker km and km per litre.
  - Duplicate merge share (after T1).
  - Gini, Jain index, worst-section fill rate.
  - Unused capacity.
  - Report where JL is worse (usually km).
